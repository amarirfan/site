The long way here

I did not study design. I studied international relations in Yogyakarta, and before that I ran my first business at school age: designing and producing custom schoolwear in Bengkulu, handling everything from the artwork to the shipping. I did not know the word yet, but that was the whole job I do now. Understand people, make the thing, stand behind it.

The route since then has not been straight. I designed multimedia for the Indonesian Embassy in Zagreb. I joined Hostinger as a graphic designer drawing illustrations for a global blog, then crossed into product design on hPanel, the control panel serving more than two million users. At Niagahoster I was the first interface designer on the team and helped build the design practice from zero. At Juvare I spent years on emergency response software, where the user might be coordinating the worst day of a city's life, and co-led a design system across seven products. Today I am a senior experience designer at Cognizant in Vilnius, leading design for an enterprise AI platform and working with clients in pharma and global finance.

Somewhere along that route, Indonesia became the place I am from and Lithuania became the place I live. My family and I made the Baltics home. The distance between those two points, cultural, linguistic, professional, is probably the most useful design education I ever received. You learn to never assume the user thinks like you when you have spent a decade being the foreigner in the room.

What I believe about the work is simple. Constraints are not the enemy of good design, they are the proof of it. Anyone can make a beautiful screen with no rules. The craft shows when medical review, legacy systems, regulation, or a two million user habit is pushing back.

Outside the work: I run long distances, slowly and stubbornly. I follow Formula 1 for the same reason I love design systems, it is precision under pressure. And I make things with my hands whenever the screen lets go of me. More on that below.
